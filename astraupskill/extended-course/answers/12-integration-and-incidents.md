# Review notes: relational campaign and incident dossiers

The relational campaign needs a verified disposable target, fresh-context durable reads and explicit provider settings. It should inspect the whole saved graph and inject a failure that actually reaches the persistence boundary. Throwing before SaveChanges does not demonstrate rollback. Unauthorized, missing and no-match cases have different expected records and should be tested separately.

In dossier A, lexical score explains ordering but does not certify freshness or authority. A source filter or warning needs an explicit metadata policy and evaluation. In dossier B, thirty matches and twenty IDs can be intended truncation; separate reads may also have weaker snapshot correspondence. The sample is recent-first, not statistically random.

Dossier C requires a capability explanation: stored recommendations exist, while decision and execution contracts remain proposed in the inspected paths. Dossier D needs a selected-run policy and identity, not accidental last-save behavior. Dossier E needs command identity to separate response recovery from a deliberate rerun. Changed input under the same key must have defined behavior.

A good operator handoff preserves facts, missing evidence and safe next observations. It does not convert Completed into solved, Pending into approved or Processed into externally delivered. Each status has a local contract that must be traced to executable code and evidence.
